# STANDARD — common

**Applies to:** every format. Read this, then your format's own `STANDARD-<format>.md`.
**Compiled:** 2026-08-24 (phase E0)

Derived rules, not source material. Every French example below was written fresh for this file. See [README.md](README.md).

---

## 1. What this file is for

An author who has read the blueprint knows *how many* questions to write and *what document type* each sits on. This file is what turns that into an item somebody can actually be scored on: how long the stimulus should be, what the question may ask, and what makes a wrong option wrong.

Two working rules govern everything below:

> **An item must be unanswerable without its stimulus.**
> **A distractor must be false for a statable reason.**

Almost every defect in a bad exam item is a violation of one of those two.

---

## 2. Length and lexis envelopes

The band of an item is a property of the **stimulus plus the task together**, not of the vocabulary alone. A simple text with an inference question is not an A2 item.

| Band | Stimulus length | Sentence ceiling | Grammar in play | Lexis | Speech rate (audio) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 25–60 words | 10 words | présent, impératif, il y a, basic questions | ~top 1000, concrete | ≤110 wpm |
| A2 | 60–110 words | 14 words | + passé composé, futur proche, one level of subordination (que, parce que, quand) | ~top 2000, concrete | ~120 wpm |
| B1 | 110–180 words | 20 words | + imparfait, futur simple, conditionnel présent, relatives, si-clauses (types 1–2) | ~top 4000, some abstract | ~140 wpm |
| B2 | 180–280 words | 28 words | + subjonctif présent, plus-que-parfait, passive, concession, hypothesis, nominalisation | ~top 8000, abstract nouns, stance markers | ~160 wpm |
| C1 | 280–400 words | no ceiling | + subjonctif passé, conditionnel passé, participe présent, gérondif, marked word order | low-frequency, specialised, figurative | ~175 wpm |
| C2 | 400+ words | no ceiling | literary and academic syntax, inversion, ellipsis, free indirect style | idiom, allusion, register play | natural, unmodified |

Use the envelope as a target, not a hard gate. A 185-word text is not thereby B2. What makes it B2 is that understanding it requires holding a position across sentences.

### 2.1 Variety of French

Standard metropolitan French is the default for every format, because both exam bodies are French. Canadian and Québécois **subject matter** is welcome and is a genuine differentiator for our candidates (the corpus already carries a `quebec-et-francophonie` theme). Canadian **lexical or phonetic variety** is not: do not write `magasiner`, `chum`, or a Québécois accent into an exam stimulus and expect it to be testing the same thing the real paper tests.

## 3. Question-stem taxonomy

Every closed item uses one of these nine stem types. Tag it. The mix is what makes a section feel like an exam rather than a comprehension worksheet.

| # | Stem type | Asks | Earliest band |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Gist / purpose | what the document is for, or what it is mainly about | A1 |
| S2 | Specific detail | a fact stated once in the document | A1 |
| S3 | Inference | something true of the document but not stated in it | B1 |
| S4 | Stance / attitude | what the speaker or writer thinks or feels about it | B1 |
| S5 | Lexical substitution | which expression could replace a word or phrase as used here | A2 |
| S6 | Discourse function | what a sentence or paragraph is *doing* (conceding, exemplifying, qualifying) | B2 |
| S7 | Grammatical form | which form completes the sentence correctly | A1 |
| S8 | Collocation | which word the surrounding words select for | A2 |
| S9 | Cohesive device | which connector or reference expression the text needs here | B1 |

S7 to S9 belong to gap-fill blocks. S1 to S6 belong to comprehension blocks.

### 3.1 Mix targets per comprehension block

| | S1 gist | S2 detail | S3 inference | S4 stance | S5 lexical | S6 discourse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1–A2 items | 30% | 55% | 0% | 5% | 10% | 0% |
| B1 items | 20% | 40% | 20% | 10% | 10% | 0% |
| B2 items | 10% | 25% | 25% | 20% | 10% | 10% |
| C1–C2 items | 5% | 15% | 30% | 25% | 10% | 15% |

A section that is 80% S2 is a memory test. A section with no S2 at all is unmoored: detail retrieval is what confirms the candidate actually processed the document.

### 3.2 Stem writing rules

- Ask in French, in the register of the paper, never in the register of the stimulus. A stem about a slangy vox-pop is still neutral French.
- The stem must be answerable from the document alone. If the stem needs the options to make sense, it is not a stem.
- Never let the stem's grammar select the key. If three options are infinitives and the key is a noun, the item is broken before anyone reads the text.
- Negative stems (`Lequel n'est PAS…`) at most once per block, with the negation typographically marked.

## 4. Distractor design

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| D1 | **Every distractor is false for a reason you can state in one sentence.** Write the reason down. If you cannot, the distractor is decoration and the item is a 1-in-2 guess dressed as 1-in-4. |
| D2 | One distractor should be a **lexical echo**: it reuses a salient word from the stimulus in a relation the stimulus does not assert. |
| D3 | One distractor should be **true of the world but not of the document**. This is what separates reading from knowing. |
| D4 | One distractor should be a **partial truth**: the right fact with the wrong scope, time, person or quantity. |
| D5 | Options within an item stay within ±40% of each other in word count. Across a whole section, the longest option is the key no more than 30% of the time. |
| D6 | The key must not be the **only** option that reuses stimulus vocabulary, nor the only one that avoids it. |
| D7 | No `toutes ces réponses`, no `aucune de ces réponses`, no `A et B`. |
| D8 | Options must be **mutually exclusive**. If two can both be defended, the item is dead. |
| D9 | **The cover test.** Hide the stimulus. If a competent French speaker with ordinary general knowledge can pick the key, the item tests general knowledge, not comprehension. Rewrite it. |
| D10 | Distractors sit in the **same semantic category** as the key. If the key is a reason, all options are reasons; if the key is a time, all options are times. |

### 4.1 A worked illustration

Written fresh for this file, to show the shape rather than to be reused.

> **Stimulus (A2, public notice):** « La bibliothèque municipale sera fermée du 3 au 10 avril pour travaux de peinture. Les documents empruntés peuvent être rendus dans la boîte de retour, à droite de l'entrée. »
>
> **Stem (S1, purpose):** Quel est le but principal de cette annonce ?
>
> | Option | Verdict | Reason |
> |---|---|---|
> | Annoncer une fermeture temporaire | **key** | stated, and it is the message's purpose |
> | Proposer de nouveaux horaires d'ouverture | false (D3) | plausible for a library notice, not in this one |
> | Signaler que la boîte de retour est déplacée | false (D2) | lexical echo: `boîte de retour` appears, but nothing is moved |
> | Inviter les lecteurs à participer aux travaux | false (D4) | partial: `travaux` is real, the invitation is not |

Note what each distractor is doing. Note that the cover test fails to identify the key: without the notice, all four are equally plausible library announcements.

## 5. Failure modes

Every item is checked against these before it leaves the author. The E9 verify stage re-checks them adversarially.

| # | Failure | How to spot it |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Two defensible keys** | Argue for each option in turn. If you can make a case for a second one, the item is dead. |
| F2 | **Answerable without the stimulus** | The cover test (D9). |
| F3 | **Giveaway by length or specificity** | The key is noticeably longer, more hedged, or more precise than the distractors. |
| F4 | **Register split** | The key paraphrases while distractors quote, or the reverse. |
| F5 | **Culture gate** | Answering needs knowledge of a French institution, holiday or brand that the stimulus does not supply. |
| F6 | **Stem leak** | The stem's grammar, number or gender selects the key. |
| F7 | **Band mismatch** | The stimulus sits in one band's envelope and the task in another. Tag it accurately; a hard question on an easy text is a hard item. |
| F8 | **Corpus collision** | The stimulus repeats a passage used elsewhere in our corpus or in another paper. Run the n-gram check. |

## 6. Authoring integrity rules

These are not style preferences. Each one is a thing that will cause a real problem if broken.

1. **Invent every proper noun.** No real people, no real companies, no real brands, no real product names. Invented ones only: `la société Verlune`, `la ville de Sainte-Ambre`, `le magasin Pralet`. This keeps us clear of defamation, trademark and the awkward case where a real business's real prices go stale.
2. **Never present an invented figure as a real statistic.** Exam stimuli are full of numbers. Ours may be invented, but they must be attributed to an invented source (`une enquête menée par l'Institut Verlune`), never to a real one, and never left floating as if they were established fact.
3. **No real exam-body names inside a stimulus.** They belong in the disclaimer, not in the content.
4. **The words `honnête`, `malhonnête`, `honnêteté` are banned** across authored content, enforced against the whole seed by `sons-alphabet.test.ts`. Argumentative stimuli reach for them constantly. Use `loyal`, `sincère`, `de bonne foi`, `intègre` instead.
5. **No em dash in candidate-facing copy.** Use a comma, a colon, or `·` as a label separator.
6. **Check every noun's gender** against `gates/data/lexique-gender.csv` rather than recalling it. Run `gates/check_gender.py` and `gates/check_french.py` before handing over.
7. **Probe the corpus with real orthography.** `corpus:probe` does not strip accents, so probing `biere` reports `bière` as absent. This is a known false-absence trap.
8. **Do not reuse a topic across papers of the same format.** The paper plan tracks which topics papers 1..N-1 consumed. Reuse makes two papers feel like one.

## 7. Open-task authoring

An open task (`pe_*`, `po_*`) is invalid without **a rubric and a model answer**. `validateExamTask` enforces this and the grading Edge Function refuses to grade without both. That is not bureaucracy: a rubric-free grade is an opinion, and a model-answer-free grade is an opinion with no anchor.

**Rubric.** Four to six criteria. Every criterion carries `maxPoints >= 1` and real `descriptors`. A zero-point criterion is something the examiner is asked to judge and then ignore, and the validator rejects it.

Our rubrics are **our own construction**. Neither exam body publishes its descriptor wording. Say so in `examinerNotes` on every open task. Do not imply the grid is theirs.

**Model answer.** Written to actually score at the task's target band, not above it. A C1 model answer on a B2 task teaches the candidate the wrong target and makes the grader's anchor wrong in the same direction. Hit the band.

**Examiner notes.** At minimum: that grading is AI-assisted and anchored on this rubric and this model answer, and that any result is a practice estimate, never an equated score.

## 8. Audio authoring (CO parts)

Every listening part needs a **transcript**, and the transcript is the thing that gets rendered. Consequences:

- Write it as speech, not as prose read aloud. Speech has false starts, fillers (`euh`, `bon`, `alors`), self-repair and elision. A transcript with none of these renders as an audiobook and is easier than the real paper.
- Mark speakers explicitly as a turn list. The render pipeline stitches per turn.
- Keep each part inside its band's speech-rate envelope (§2). Rate is a real difficulty lever and the cheapest one we control.
- Name the register in the part's authoring note so casting can match it. A vox-pop, a station announcement and a radio feature are three different voices.
- **The transcript is never shown in exam conditions.** It exists for rendering, for review, and for the practice-mode transcript toggle. If a part's audio fails, the part is marked unscored; it does not silently become a reading item.

## 9. Handover checklist

Per item:

- [ ] Band tagged, and the stimulus sits in that band's envelope
- [ ] Stem type tagged from S1–S9, and the block's mix target is met
- [ ] Key is unique and defensible; the argument for each distractor's falsity is written down
- [ ] Cover test passed (D9)
- [ ] Option lengths within ±40%; key is not the longest by default
- [ ] `check_french.py` and `check_gender.py` clean
- [ ] No banned words, no real proper nouns, no unattributed figures
- [ ] n-gram check clean against our corpus and other papers
- [ ] `targetItemIds` populated **per item** against real corpus items

Per open task:

- [ ] Rubric with 4–6 criteria, each with `maxPoints >= 1` and real descriptors
- [ ] Model answer written at the target band, not above it
- [ ] `examinerNotes` state the rubric is ours and the result is a practice estimate
- [ ] `responseSpec` bounds set, so "too short" is markable

Per CO part:

- [ ] Transcript written as speech, as a turn list
- [ ] Speech rate inside the band envelope
- [ ] Register named for casting
- [ ] `playCount` and `readWindowS` set from the blueprint
- [ ] `imageAlt` present wherever `imageRef` is

## Appendix — the gender gate's four false-positive classes

`gates/check_prose_gender.py` is deliberately conservative but it is not
silent. Run across all five TEF papers it reports 24 disagreements on 5,793
determiner+noun pairs, and every one of them is a false positive of one of
four kinds. Read this before "fixing" any of them.

1. **An object pronoun read as a determiner.** `on nous le demande`, `tout le
   monde le paie`, `ce qui le forme`, `on le présente`, `le formule sans
   détour`. The gate sees `le` + word and looks the word up as a noun; here
   `le` is a pronoun and the word is a verb.

2. **An epicene adjective between determiner and noun.** `une autre
   information`, `la double authentification`. The gate requires the noun to
   follow the determiner immediately, and `autre` and `double` are the two
   that most often sit in between. It looks *them* up instead.

3. **A feminised agent noun Lexique does not carry.** `la maire` is current,
   correct French for a woman holding the office; Lexique lists `maire` as
   masculine only. Same shape as the pairs-only feminisation rule settled for
   the A2 band.

4. **A noun whose modern sense Lexique does not have.** `le Canada` (the gate
   finds a feminine homograph), `la box` for an internet router, `un doudou`.

What this appendix is NOT: a licence to skip the gate. The classes above are
recognisable in one reading each, and anything outside them is a real finding.
