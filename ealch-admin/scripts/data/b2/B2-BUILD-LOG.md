## How B2 teaches complex structures, skills and exam practice

B2 is where a learner stops producing single correct sentences and starts
controlling LONGER French: a paragraph that holds together, a text read for its
stance, a recording followed at natural speed. Every lesson keeps the house
shape (acts, one quiz with drills, sheets, a graded task); what changes is the
length and the layering inside it.

### Complex structures (Units 1 to 3)

Each grammar act runs **form → meaning → choice → text**:

1. **Form**, briefly, and only what B1 did not build (b2.01 builds the
   plus-que-parfait; b2.05 builds the subjonctif passé). A recap sheet links
   back to the B1 lesson for the rest.
2. **Meaning in contrast.** The structure beside the one it is confused with:
   plus-que-parfait vs passé composé on one timeline, « bien que » vs « même
   si », « avant que » + subjunctive vs « après que » + indicative. The
   contrast is the unit of teaching, not the form alone.
3. **Choice under load.** Checks with two or three structures live at once,
   in multi-clause sentences (« Si j'avais su qu'il serait parti avant que tu
   arrives… »). Error-spot rounds use the real learner errors (« si
   j'aurais », « après qu'il soit »).
4. **In a text.** Every act from 3 on ends with the structure inside a
   paragraph: reorder, complete, or rewrite a 60 to 120 word text so it holds.

Recognition lessons (b2.08, b2.09) run **spot → map → understand**: find the
form in a real literary extract, map it to its infinitive and to the tense a
learner would use today, then answer comprehension questions on the extract.
They never ask the learner to produce the form.

### Reading at B2

- **Longer texts:** 200 to 350 words (B1 was 120 to 180), real B2 genres: an
  opinion column, a report extract, a public notice, a literary page, a
  science explainer.
- **Four question types, always labelled:** global (what is it for), detail,
  inference (what is implied), and stance (what does the writer think, how
  do you know). B1 asked mostly global and detail; B2 is weighted to
  inference and stance.
- **Glossaries shrink:** words a B2 reader should infer are NOT glossed; a
  « Devine le sens » check comes first, as b1.37 taught.

### Listening at B2

- **Natural speed**, 1 to 3 minutes: interviews, radio debates, a lecture
  extract, a phone call with overlaps and repairs. Hidden transcripts by
  default; the transcript is revealed after the questions.
- **Listen for structure and stance** (b2.25's strategy): who agrees with
  whom, where the turn comes, what is fact and what is opinion.
- TTS speaks every line (fr-FR). Recordings with real regional voices are a
  later upgrade; the studio briefs in each lesson say which lines need them.

### Speaking and writing (production)

- **Writing tasks grow:** 150 to 250 words at B2 (B1 was 80 to 150), with the
  B2 rubric (task, coherence and cohesion, range, accuracy, register). The
  model answer is annotated to show where each criterion is met.
- **Speaking** uses the existing `scenario` role play (interaction) and a
  prepared monologue in the task (« présente et défends… »), self-checked
  against a model. There is no live speech grading yet.
- **Every task names the B2 structures it expects** (a counterargument with
  « certes… mais », a regret with the conditionnel passé), so a unit's grammar
  is used in its skills.

### Practice and recycling

- **Spaced reuse:** each lesson's quiz has one round that recycles structures
  from EARLIER B2 lessons (named in the round), so Unit 3 keeps Unit 1 alive.
- **Items go to review** as in B1 (« words sent to review »): every structure
  row and collocation lands in the learner's SRS deck.
- **Mixed drills per unit** come from the per-round remediation drills already
  in the house shape; no new section type is needed.

### Exam practice

- **The four exam-target lessons** (b2.23 PE, b2.24 CE, b2.25 CO, b2.26 PO)
  each end on a task in the exact format of a DELF B2 / TEF / TCF item, graded
  against that exam's grid, and `EXAM_PREP_TARGETS` sends a learner who fails
  that skill in the Examiner to the lesson.
- **Every other B2 lesson** has one exam-style round in its quiz (a DELF-style
  CE question, a TCF-style gap fill) so the exam format is familiar before
  the learner reaches a paper.
- **The Examiner stays the place for full papers** (15 published: TEF, TCF,
  DELF blanc 01 to 05). Lessons prepare; papers measure.
- **Open question for Paul:** a « Bilan B2 » (a closing review-and-exam lesson,
  as a2.35 is for A2) is not in the 34. Add it as b2.35?

## Wave 0: enablers, before the first B2 lesson

1. **Make the import pipeline band-aware.** It is B1-only today:
   `b1-import.logic.ts` writes every unit as `level: 'b1'` (`unitBody`), the
   floor checks look for `fr.b1` rows, `B1_SLUGS` is the only lesson list, and
   `_preview-b1.ts`, the three b1 house tests, `b1-import-readiness.ts` and
   `scratch/_app-checks-b1.mts` all read B1 only. Add a band (`--band b2`, a
   `B2_SLUGS` list in `data/b2/`), keep the B1 behaviour byte-identical, and
   prove it by re-running the B1 dry run (0 changes) before any B2 import.
2. **Route the exam skills.** Add `PE@b2 → b2.23`, `CE@b2 → b2.24`,
   `CO@b2 → b2.25`, `PO@b2 → b2.26` to `EXAM_PREP_TARGETS`
   (`ealch-v2/src/store/progress.logic.ts`) when those lessons publish.
3. **Ship the 80 MiB ceiling to phones.** Installs that predate the raise
   refuse any snapshot past 50 MiB (52.4 MB). v76 is 39.9 MB; B2 at B1 density
   adds ~0.3 MB per lesson, so the old ceiling is crossed around b2.30. The raise
   must reach users (OTA or binary) before then.
4. **Decide what B2 bundles in the app** (`SEED_CUT`). B1 is bundled (Paul,
   2026-09-27). Bundling B2 adds ~11 MB to the binary; not bundling it means B2
   arrives by download, which the paywall already implies (B2 is not a free
   band). A band must be all-in or all-out: a partial band draws a trail with
   holes.
5. **Settle a2.36 « Les pronoms toniques »** (drafted, never applied). b2.11
   emphatic pronouns assumes the stressed pronouns were taught.
6. **Art:** Paul makes the art himself in ChatGPT from each lesson's
   `-image-prompts.md` (about 370 images for B2). Give him the prompt links and
   save folders; do not use Fal.

## What is different at B2

- **The French stops being simplified.** B1 wrote "simple French instructions";
  B2 instructions and texts are natural French at B2 difficulty. English is
  STILL authored on every learner string (the EN pill hides it by default).
  The learner is still tu.
- **Build on B1, never re-teach it.** Many B2 lessons deepen a B1 one: b2.01 (b1.03, b1.04, b1.19), b2.02-04
  (b1.08, b1.10), b2.06 (b1.15, b1.16), b2.15 (b1.31), b2.16 (b1.32), b2.21
  (b1.20), b2.24 (b1.37), b2.25 (b1.38), b2.22-23 (b1.39), b2.26 (b1.36), b2.34
  (b1.27-29). Act 1 or 2 names the B1 lesson in words (« la leçon 31 du B1 »),
  reuses its published rows by id, and then goes past it.
- **Recognition lessons** (b2.08 passé simple, b2.09 literary subjunctive):
  the learner recognises and maps, never produces. Quizzes test recognition; the
  task is a comprehension or rewrite task, not free production of the form.
- **Exam-target lessons** (b2.23-26) mirror the B2 exam grids (DELF B2, TEF/TCF
  NCLC 7-8) in their task rubric, as b1.39 mirrored B1 PE.
- **Ids:** `fr.b2.<theme>.1000` up, per theme, probed first; the level is part
  of the id, so compare full ids. Postgres holds 7,521 published b2 rows over 66
  themes and no b2 unit yet.
- **Cite by trail position** (« la leçon 23 du B2 »), never a raw id.

## How one wave runs (unchanged from B1)

1. Set up themes in Postgres (probe, pick the home, sub-themes, claim a 1000+
   block) and record them in the ledger below.
2. One fresh agent per lesson, background, touching only its own four files.
   Each validates act by act and runs the app checks to 0.
3. Verify each lesson, update the status table, commit the wave.
4. Paul makes the art from the prompt files; register it with alt text.
5. At a gate: preview on the Pixel, Paul reviews.
6. Import: trial merge (whole app suite on a trial seed) → `--apply` → merge
   into the seed → both test suites → publish dry run → publish with Paul's OK.
